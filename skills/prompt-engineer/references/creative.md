# Creative & Conversational Applications

Additional principles for prompts where the output is *experienced* rather than consumed — narrative fiction, roleplay, chatbot persona, aesthetic writing.

These supplement the core Writing Principles. Apply them alongside, not instead of, the main skill.

## Narrative & Fiction

When intended outcome involves narrative, fiction, or aesthetic output:

1. **Concrete Foundation**: Physical/sensory detail must precede abstraction, metaphor, and emotional interpretation. Most commonly violated principle.
2. **Pacing**: Interleave description with dialogue, monologue, and short actions. Highest-leverage principle for narrative vividness.
3. **Character Voice**: Dialogue and monologue are the primary vehicles for personality, emotion, and engagement.
4. **Sensory Breadth**: End AI defaults to visual-heavy description. Direct inclusion of non-visual senses (sound, touch, smell) with quality/intensity modifiers.

## Conversational Persona

When prompting chatbots, assistants, or any system with a persistent voice:

- **Behavior over labels**: "You are a cheerful assistant" produces generic output. Specify *how* the persona thinks and acts — decision patterns, verbal habits, what it notices and ignores. Contextualize viewpoint and knowledge rather than declaring a role.
- **Voice consistency**: Define the persona's register, sentence rhythm, and vocabulary boundaries. End AI drifts toward its default voice over long conversations — anchor with concrete speech patterns, not abstract adjectives.
- **Format leakage**: The prompt's own tone, structure, and style bleed into output (Format Synchrony). A formal, clinical prompt produces a formal, clinical persona regardless of instructions to be casual. Write the prompt *in* the voice you want.

## Constraint Caution

Excessive constraints are especially harmful for creative and conversational output. Numbered steps rigidify reasoning and degrade creative Performance — reserve procedural sequences for output structure, not creative process.

Principles have systemic effects beyond their stated scope. A pacing improvement may also improve character voice; a narrator-editorializing ban may also suppress dialogue. Evaluate by systemic impact, not stated intent.
